The History of Jaro Cathedral

Discover the important events that shaped one of Iloilo's best-known religious and historical landmarks.

A Story of Faith and Resilience

Jaro Cathedral has a long history connected with the development of the Catholic Church in Iloilo. It is dedicated to St. Elizabeth of Hungary and is also the National Shrine of Our Lady of Candles.

The cathedral has experienced natural disasters and restoration over the years. Its continuing role as a place of worship reflects the devotion and resilience of the local community.

Historical Timeline

Key dates recorded in historical references for the cathedral and its separate bell tower.

1744 — Early Bell Tower

The early history of Jaro's bell tower dates to the Spanish colonial period. It was built under Augustinian leadership and later suffered damage from earthquakes.

1874 — Construction of the Cathedral

The cathedral was built under the direction of Mariano Cuartero, O.P., the first bishop of Jaro. This date is recorded on the National Historical Commission of the Philippines' historical marker.

1948 — Earthquake Damage

A major earthquake in January 1948 damaged the cathedral. The separate bell tower also suffered serious structural damage during the earthquake.

1956 — Restoration

The cathedral was repaired under the direction of Jose Ma. Cuenco, the first archbishop of Jaro, following the earthquake damage.

1984 — National Historical Recognition

The Jaro bell tower was declared a National Historical Landmark, recognizing its importance to Philippine history and heritage.

Today — A Living Heritage

Jaro Cathedral continues to serve the faithful and remains a landmark for visitors interested in the history and culture of Iloilo City.

Preserving the Past

Historical buildings help communities understand their origins and preserve traditions. Jaro Cathedral remains an important example of Iloilo's religious and architectural heritage.
